What open and click rates measure

Open rate is unique opens divided by delivered emails — it measures inbox placement and subject-line appeal. Click rate (unique click-through rate) is unique clicks divided by delivered emails — it measures whether the content, offer and call-to-action did their job. Great opens with weak clicks usually means the subject line over-promised or the CTA was buried.

Why open rates are noisy: Apple Mail Privacy Protection

Since late 2021, Apple Mail Privacy Protection pre-loads email images — including tracking pixels — for many Apple Mail users. The widely reported effect is inflated open rates, recorded even when nobody looked. With an iOS-heavy audience, treat opens as a rough trend line and put your weight on clicks and conversions.

How to read benchmarks

Benchmarks only work when you compare like with like: the same list type, the same audience and the same definitions (unique opens vs total opens changes everything). Worked example

A SaaS sender reports a 34% open rate and a 2.1% click rate. Against the illustrative SaaS benchmarks (32% open / 2.8% click): opens are above, clicks are below — verdict Mixed. Delivery and subject lines look fine; the weak clicks point to a content or CTA problem, not a deliverability problem. Fixing this means testing the offer, copy and CTA placement, not sending more emails.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing across industries. Nonprofit and media benchmarks sit well above ecommerce — the wrong peer group makes a healthy list look broken.
  • Ignoring list hygiene. Dead addresses and chronic non-openers drag down both rates and sender reputation. Clean the list before you trust the comparison.
  • Optimizing opens alone. Clickbait subject lines lift opens while hurting clicks and unsubscribes. Opens get you noticed; clicks pay the bills.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good email open rate?

It depends on your industry and audience. The illustrative benchmarks in this tool range from about 28% to 38% opens across industries, but those are examples, not targets. Judge your own rate against your past performance and same-industry figures from your ESP, and remember that opens are a noisy metric since Apple Mail Privacy Protection.

Why did my open rates jump recently?

The widely reported cause is Apple Mail Privacy Protection (MPP). Apple pre-loads email images — including the tracking pixel that records an open — for many Apple Mail users, which inflates open rates without any real engagement. If your audience is iOS-heavy, expect permanently higher opens and lean on clicks and conversions instead.

Open rate vs click rate — which matters?

Click rate matters more. An open only proves the email rendered; a click proves the content was interesting enough to act on, and it is far less distorted by privacy pre-loading. Benchmark your engagement on clicks, and use sudden open-rate drops — not the absolute level — to spot deliverability problems.

How often should I clean my list?

Remove hard bounces immediately and run a full hygiene pass every quarter. Suppress subscribers who have not opened or clicked in 6–12 months (or try a re-engagement win-back first). A smaller, engaged list beats a bloated one: better deliverability, cleaner metrics and lower ESP bills.

Are these benchmarks accurate for my business?

No — the benchmarks built into this tool are explicitly illustrative, included so you can see how the comparison works. Real benchmarks vary by ESP methodology, list size, geography and how "click rate" is defined. Verify any comparison against your own ESP data before acting on it.
