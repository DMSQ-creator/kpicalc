CAC & LTV Benchmarks 2025

Published: October 2026  |  Next review: October 2027

Primary data: Benchmarkit 2026 B2B SaaS & AI-Native Performance Metrics (342 companies, calendar 2025)

The 2025 medians: a blended CAC ratio of $1.30, a 16-month CAC payback, and a 4.1x lifetime-value-to-CAC ratio. Acquisition got cheaper year over year — retention, not acquisition, is where the market deteriorated.

Formulas

CAC = Total sales & marketing spend / New customers acquired
LTV = ARPU × Gross margin % / Monthly churn rate
LTV:CAC = LTV / CAC
CAC payback (months) = CAC / (Average new MRR per customer × Gross margin %)

The most common error is undercounting CAC: ad spend only, without sales salaries, tools, agencies and events, makes every ratio look artificially good. Count fully loaded costs.

The 3:1 rule — and its limits

The industry rule of thumb, from the Bessemer / David Skok lineage of SaaS metrics, is simple:

  • Below 1:1 — you lose money on every customer. Unsustainable.
  • 1:1 to 3:1 — marginal. The business works but leaves little room for error.
  • 3:1 or higher — healthy. The widely cited bar for a sustainable SaaS business.
  • Above 5:1 — elite, but possibly a sign you are underinvesting in growth.

Benchmarkit's 2025 median of 4.1x sits comfortably above the bar; its top quartile reaches 7.8x. Pair the ratio with payback: under 12 months is excellent, 12–18 months is sustainable, beyond 24 months is a red flag that warrants restructuring go-to-market. A good ratio with a terrible payback still breaks cash flow.

Stage targets shift too: roughly 1.5:1–2:1 at seed, tightening to at least 3:1 by Series B, with payback expectations tightening from under 18 months to under 12 months as companies mature (SaaSHero 2026).

One number is never the story. A 4.1x ratio means little if churn is about to cut customer lifetime in half. Read this report alongside our SaaS churn benchmarks — churn sets lifetime, lifetime sets LTV, and LTV decides whether your CAC is cheap or expensive.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good LTV:CAC ratio?

The widely used rule of thumb is 3:1 or higher — each customer should generate at least three times what they cost to acquire. Below 1:1 the business loses money on every customer. Above 5:1 is excellent but can signal underinvestment in growth. Benchmarkit's 2026 survey found a median CLTV:CAC of 4.1x for calendar 2025.

What is a good CAC payback period?

Under 12 months is excellent and 12 to 18 months is sustainable for most SaaS businesses; beyond 24 months is a red flag. Benchmarkit's 2026 survey found a median CAC payback of 16 months for calendar 2025, with a top quartile of 6 months or less.

How do you calculate CAC payback?

CAC payback equals CAC divided by the monthly gross margin generated by a new customer: CAC / (average new MRR per customer x gross margin %). Count fully loaded sales and marketing costs — salaries, tools, agencies, events — not just ad spend, or the result looks artificially good.

Why does CAC vary so much by company?

Sales motion dominates: product-led products acquire customers for a few hundred dollars, mid-market sales-led companies for a few thousand, and enterprise deals for tens of thousands. Contract size matters too — Benchmarkit found median payback of 11 months under $5,000 annual contract value but 22 months at $50,000 to $100,000.
