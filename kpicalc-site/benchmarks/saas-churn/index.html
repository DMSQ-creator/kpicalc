SaaS Churn Benchmarks 2025

Published: October 2026  |  Next review: October 2027

Primary data: ChartMogul SaaS Retention Report (December 2025, ~3,500 companies above $250k ARR)  |  Benchmarkit 2026 B2B SaaS & AI-Native Metrics (342 companies, calendar 2025)

The 2025 story is a split market: traditional B2B SaaS holds up, while AI-native products leak revenue at more than twice the rate — unless they charge real prices. The single most important habit: compare yourself to your own segment, never to a blended "average SaaS" number.

Definitions first

Logo churn counts accounts lost; revenue churn counts dollars lost. They diverge when churned customers are smaller or larger than average — losing many small accounts hurts logo churn more than revenue churn, and vice versa.

GRR = (Starting MRR − Churned MRR − Contraction MRR) / Starting MRR × 100
NRR = (Starting MRR + Expansion MRR − Churned MRR − Contraction MRR) / Starting MRR × 100

GRR excludes expansion and caps at 100%: how leaky the bucket is. NRR includes expansion and can exceed 100%: whether expansion refills the leak. Annual churn converts to monthly churn with compounding, not division:

Monthly churn ≈ 1 − (1 − Annual churn)^(1/12)

A 30% annual churn is roughly 2.9% monthly, not 2.5%.

Cross-check — Benchmarkit 2026

Benchmarkit's survey of 342 B2B SaaS and AI-native companies (calendar 2025) paints a healthier picture — because its sample skews larger and more enterprise than ChartMogul's platform data.

MetricMedianTop quartileSample
Gross revenue retention84%91%226
Net revenue retention102%110%230

Source: Benchmarkit 2026. The direction matters as much as the level: GRR fell four points from 88% in the prior report — Benchmarkit calls it the most significant single-year deterioration in its four-year trend. By contract size, GRR runs from 80% below $5,000 ACV to 91% at $50,000–$100,000 ACV. By pricing model, NRR is 108% for usage-based-only products vs 98% for seat-based — a cohort difference, not proof that switching models improves retention.

Why the two sources disagree — and why both are right. ChartMogul's median B2B NRR of 82% and Benchmarkit's 102% describe different populations: platform billing data including small self-serve companies vs a survey of larger B2B SaaS. If you are a small self-serve product, ChartMogul's row is your mirror. If you are a larger sales-led company, Benchmarkit's is. The blended "average SaaS churn" you see quoted elsewhere is neither.

Churn by price point and billing period

Two older but still useful cuts. ChartMogul's 2022 data found median monthly customer churn of 6.1% for products under $25 a month and 2.2% above $500 a month — price predicts churn better than almost anything else. And ChartMogul's 2025 billing data shows annual billing retains better at every price point, with the biggest gap where budgets are smallest:

Price bandMeasureAnnual billingMonthly billing
Under $25 ARPACustomer retention62%41%

Source: ChartMogul SaaS Billing Report 2025. Moving a low-priced product to annual billing is one of the largest retention levers available — but it raises the acquisition bar and defers revenue, so it is not free. For context, Vitally's 2025 B2B SaaS benchmarks put median monthly churn at 3.5% (2.6% voluntary + 0.8% involuntary).

Frequently asked questions

What is a good SaaS churn rate?

It depends on price point and segment. ChartMogul's 2022 data found median monthly customer churn of 6.1% for products under $25 a month and 2.2% above $500 a month. For revenue, Benchmarkit's 2026 survey found median gross revenue retention of 84% and net revenue retention of 102% for calendar 2025. Judge against your own segment, not a single blended average.

What is the difference between GRR and NRR?

Gross revenue retention excludes expansion and caps at 100% — it measures how leaky the bucket is. Net revenue retention includes expansion and can exceed 100% — it measures whether expansion refills the leak. NRR above 100% means the existing customer base grows without any new customers.

Why do ChartMogul and Benchmarkit retention figures disagree?

Sample composition. ChartMogul's data comes from billing data on its platform, including many small self-serve companies (median B2B NRR 82% in 2025). Benchmarkit's figures come from a survey of larger B2B SaaS companies (median NRR 102%). Read the row that matches your segment, not the global average.

How do you convert annual churn to monthly churn?

Churn compounds, so it is not a simple divide-by-12. Approximate monthly churn as 1 minus (1 minus annual churn) to the power of 1/12. A 30% annual churn is roughly 2.9% monthly, not 2.5%.
